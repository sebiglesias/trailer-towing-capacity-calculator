Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a truck run out of payload capacity before reaching its maximum tow rating?
Auto manufacturers advertise maximum towing ratings assuming an empty base-model truck with only a 150-lb driver. In real life, your trailer's tongue weight (10% to 15% of trailer weight) sits directly in the truck bed, consuming your truck's payload capacity. Adding 4 passengers, luggage, and a heavy tongue weight almost always exceeds the truck's Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR) long before hitting the advertised tow limit.
What is the proper tongue weight percentage for safe towing?
For standard bumper-pull travel trailers and cargo haulers, tongue weight must be between **10% and 15% of the total loaded trailer weight** (12% is ideal). If tongue weight is below 10%, the trailer will violently sway and fishtail at highway speeds. If tongue weight exceeds 15%, the rear suspension sags, lifting weight off the front steering tires.
When is a Weight Distribution Hitch (WDH) required?
Most Class IV truck receiver hitches require a Weight Distribution Hitch for any trailer weighing over 5,000 lbs. A WDH uses spring bars to leverage and transfer a portion of the heavy tongue weight forward to the truck's front steering axle and back to the trailer's axles, keeping the vehicle completely level.
